## 1. The project in one page

- **Question (fictional).** Did a 2015 state R&D tax credit raise patenting among
  eligible firms?
- **Setting.** Simulated balanced panel of 2,000 firms, 2010–2019 (20,000 firm-years).
  About half the firms are eligible ("treated") from 2015 on.
- **Design.** Difference-in-differences with firm and year fixed effects (two-way fixed
  effects, TWFE); event study around 2015 with 2014 as the reference year, treated vs.
  never-treated firms. Standard errors clustered by firm.
- **Headline numbers** (from `output/main_results.csv`):
  - Patent count, OLS in levels: **0.324** (SE 0.038) additional patents per firm-year
    post-credit; control-group pre-period mean 1.247.
  - Any patent, linear probability model (LPM): **0.072** (SE 0.012); control pre-period
    mean 0.579.
  - Event-study pre-period coefficients (2010–2013) all within 1.01 SE of zero;
    post-period coefficients 0.26–0.35.
- **Status.** Main spec run and logged. The appendix Poisson spec (PPML) is not yet
  implemented (backlog B2). No robustness battery yet.

## 2. Why the question matters

In this toy setting the motivation is the generic one for place-based R&D credits: do
tax subsidies to R&D change innovative output, and on which margin — more patents from
already-patenting firms (intensive) or more firms patenting at all (extensive)? The
design separates the two by estimating the count in levels and the any-patent indicator
side by side. No real policy is studied.

## 3. Data

All data are generated by `code/simulate.py` → `data/panel.csv` (gitignored; regenerate,
never hand-edit).

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Unit | firm (`firm_id`) |
| Years | 2010–2019, balanced |
| Firms / firm-years | 2,000 / 20,000 |
| Treatment assignment | each firm eligible with probability 0.5 (exact arm counts are not recorded in any document of record) |
| Event year | 2015 (`post` = treated × year ≥ 2015) |
| Outcomes | `patents` (count), `any_patent` = 1{patents > 0} |
| Seed | `SEED = 20261023` |

**Data-generating process.** Patents are Poisson draws with rate
exp(−0.2 + firm effect + 0.03·(year − 2010) + 0.25·post), firm effects ~ N(0, 0.8²).
The built-in treatment effect is **`EFFECT = 0.25` on the log rate** — i.e. roughly a
28% increase in the expected count. Every logged number depends on SEED and EFFECT;
they do not change without a logged decision.

## 4. Research design in detail

**Main specification** (ratified 2026-10-23, `docs/key-decisions.md`):

  y_it = β · post_it + α_i + γ_t + ε_it

estimated for y = `patents` (OLS in levels) and y = `any_patent` (LPM), with firm (α_i)
and year (γ_t) fixed effects and CR1 standard errors clustered by firm.

- **Why levels + LPM, not log(1+patents):** counts with many zeros; levels and the
  extensive margin stay interpretable, while log(1+x) depends on units and has no clean
  interpretation. log(1+x) is banned as a main spec (project and user-level convention).
- **PPML** (Poisson pseudo-maximum likelihood) goes in the appendix. It is the spec
  that targets the log-rate `EFFECT` directly — so it doubles as a check that simulation
  and estimator agree. Not yet implemented (B2).
- **Estimator.** A hand-rolled within estimator (numpy/pandas). Two-way demeaning is
  exact only in a balanced panel, so `code/estimate.py` asserts balance; that assert
  must not be dropped. PPML will need its own fixed-effect handling, not the demeaning.
- **Event study.** `patents` on treated × year dummies (2014 omitted), firm + year FE,
  treated vs. never-treated firms, firm-clustered SEs. There is a single event-based group
  compared against never-treated firms, so the convention requiring both main rel_year
  effects and treatment × rel_year interactions (which applies to two event-based groups)
  does not bind here.
- **Sample screens.** None beyond the balanced simulated panel.

**Precision hierarchy.**
- *Precise:* both main coefficients (t ≈ 8.5 for patents, ≈ 6.1 for any_patent).
- *Point-stable:* individual post-period event-study coefficients — each ≈ 3.2–4.6 SE
  from zero, range 0.26–0.35, no visible trend in event time.
- *Pre-trends:* largest |coef/SE| is rel_year −3 at ≈ 1.01. No joint pre-trend test has
  been run or logged.

**Interpretation caveat.** The levels coefficient (0.324 patents) is **not** comparable
to `EFFECT = 0.25`, which is a log-rate effect in the DGP. Only PPML (B2) puts the
estimate on the scale of `EFFECT`.

## 8. Known artifacts and bug postmortems

**CSV precision mismatch (found and fixed 2026-10-23).**
- *What was wrong:* `code/estimate.py` wrote output CSVs with `float_format="%.4f"`,
  while the results log cited six decimals (e.g. coef 0.323959). The CSVs could not
  reproduce the logged digits.
- *How it was caught:* first session's handoff, cross-checking the results log
  against the CSVs.
- *Fix:* `float_format` dropped from both `to_csv` calls (`4c397c5`); outputs
  regenerated. Ratified as a script-default change in `docs/key-decisions.md`.
- *What died:* nothing. Every old 4-dp value equals the new full-precision value
  rounded; estimates unchanged.

**Grep false positive.** `rng.poisson` in `code/simulate.py` is the DGP draw, not a
Poisson estimator. Sweeps for log1p/Poisson will hit it; ignore.
